Most people choose a cruise by price and port, then find out on day two that the ship's personality is what really shapes the trip. The Big 4 — Royal Caribbean, Carnival, Norwegian and MSC — all sail big, modern ships to the Caribbean, but they're built for different travelers.

Here's the short version, then the detail.

| If you want… | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| The most to do on board, especially with kids | Royal Caribbean |
| The lowest fare and a fun, casual crowd | Carnival |
| No schedules and lots of restaurant choice | Norwegian |
| A stylish ship at a low price, or Europe | MSC |

## Royal Caribbean: the ship is the destination

Royal builds the biggest ships at sea. The Icon class — Icon of the Seas, Star of the Seas and, since July 2026, Legend of the Seas — packs a full water park, a surf simulator, ice shows and Broadway-scale productions into one hull. The Oasis class is close behind.

**Choose it if** your group has mixed ages and mixed interests. Teens, toddlers and grandparents can all find something.

**Skip it if** you want calm. Sea days on Icon-class ships feel like a busy resort.

**Cost reality:** base fares sit in the middle of the pack, but Royal's pricing for drink packages, Wi-Fi and specialty dining moves constantly. Check your Cruise Planner often and rebook add-ons when they drop.

## Carnival: maximum fun per dollar

Carnival sails from more US ports than anyone, which often means no flight. It runs lots of 3–5 night trips, keeps fares low and leans into easygoing fun: comedy clubs, deck parties, and free eats like Guy's Burger Joint.

**Choose it if** price is the deciding factor, or you want a long-weekend getaway with friends.

**Skip it if** you want a polished, upscale atmosphere or a private suite area.

**Worth knowing:** Carnival replaced its old VIFP loyalty program with points-based Carnival Rewards on June 1, 2026. [Here's how it works](/articles/carnival-rewards-explained).

## Norwegian: your schedule, not theirs

Norwegian's Freestyle Cruising means no assigned dinner times and no formal nights. The ships carry a long list of specialty restaurants, and the newest Prima-class ships (Prima, Viva, Aqua and the 2026 Norwegian Luna) are some of the best-looking in the mainstream market.

**Choose it if** you hate being told when to eat, or you're traveling as a group of adults.

**Skip it if** you add up every extra. The headline fare plus "Free at Sea" perks plus service charges takes some math. [We break it down here](/articles/ncl-free-at-sea-explained).

## MSC: European style, aggressive prices

MSC is the fastest-growing of the four. Its World-class ships — World Europa, World America and the new World Asia — are huge and design-forward, and Caribbean fares are often the lowest you'll find for a new ship.

**Choose it if** you want a new ship for less, you're sailing the Mediterranean, or you want a true suite enclave (the MSC Yacht Club) without luxury-line prices.

**Skip it if** you expect American-style service rhythms. Dining is more structured, and European sailings are multilingual.

## How to decide in five minutes

1. **Check your nearest homeport.** If Carnival sails from your city and the others don't, that alone may decide it.
2. **Pick your one priority** — price, kids, food, or freedom — and use our [Line Matcher](/) to see who ranks first.
3. **Compare the same ship class**, not the same line. A new MSC ship and an older Royal ship are very different trips.
4. **Price the whole trip.** Add gratuities, drinks and Wi-Fi before comparing fares.

<Verdict>
For first-timers with kids, Royal Caribbean is the safest bet. For the cheapest good time, Carnival. For couples and friend groups who hate schedules, Norwegian. For the newest ship at the lowest price, MSC.
</Verdict>
