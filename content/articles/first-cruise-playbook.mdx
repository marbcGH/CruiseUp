Your first cruise is mostly easy. The few things that go wrong are predictable, and almost all of them happen before you board or at the very end. This is the checklist we give friends.

## Before you book

**1. Pick the ship before the itinerary.** Ports matter less than you think on a 7-night Caribbean trip. The ship is where you'll spend most of your time.

**2. Price the real total.** Daily gratuities (roughly $16–$25 per person per day, depending on line and cabin type), drinks, Wi-Fi and specialty dining can add 30–50% to the fare.

**3. Book a cancellable fare if you're unsure.** Non-refundable deposits save a little, but cost a lot if plans change.

<Callout type="money">
Ask your line or travel agent about repricing. If the fare drops before final payment, many lines will lower your price or give you onboard credit.
</Callout>

## Before you go

**4. Fly in the day before.** Ships leave on time whether or not your flight did.

**5. Check your documents.** A passport is strongly recommended even on "closed-loop" cruises from the US. If you miss the ship and need to fly to the next port, you'll need one.

**6. Download the line's app and do online check-in.** You'll pick your arrival time and get your boarding pass. Early arrival windows go fast.

**7. Pack a day-one bag.** Your checked luggage may not reach your cabin until evening. Keep swimwear, sunscreen, medications, chargers and documents with you.

## On board

**8. Explore on day one.** The pools and slides are quietest while everyone else is unpacking.

**9. Book shows and dining early.** On big ships, popular shows and specialty restaurants fill up. Many lines open reservations before you sail.

**10. Use airplane mode.** Ships run maritime cell networks, and roaming charges at sea can be steep. Turn cellular data off and use the ship's Wi-Fi.

**11. Your keycard is your wallet.** Everything is charged to your onboard account. Check it in the app every couple of days so there's no surprise at the end.

**12. Muster drill is mandatory.** It's usually quick: watch the safety video, then check in at your assigned station.

## In port

**13. Be back 30 minutes before "all aboard."** The ship won't wait for independent tours. Ship-sold excursions are pricier, but the ship waits for them.

**14. Private islands are easy wins.** Days at places like Perfect Day at CocoCay or Celebration Key are simple, and the included areas are good. Upcharge areas are optional.

## The last night

**15. Put out your luggage — or carry it off.** Self-assist disembarkation (taking your own bags off) is the fastest way home if you can manage your luggage.

<Verdict>
Fly in early, pack a day-one bag, book the popular stuff before you sail, and check your onboard account every couple of days. Do those four things and you'll avoid nearly every first-timer mistake.
</Verdict>
