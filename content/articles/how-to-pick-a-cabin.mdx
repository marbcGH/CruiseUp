Cabin choice affects your sleep, your budget and how you use the ship. Here's how to choose.

## The four cabin types

**Inside** — no window, lowest price. Perfect if you're rarely in the room, and the darkness helps you sleep.

**Oceanview** — a window that doesn't open. Natural light, small price bump.

**Balcony** — a private outdoor space. The most popular choice for good reason, especially on scenic routes like Alaska or Norway.

**Suite** — more space and perks. On some lines (MSC Yacht Club, NCL's The Haven, Royal's Star Class), suites come with a private area of the ship.

## Location matters more than you'd think

- **Midship, lower deck** = least motion. Best if you're worried about seasickness.
- **Aft (back) balconies** have great wake views but can feel vibration.
- **Forward cabins** feel the most motion in rough seas.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check what's above and below">
Cabins under the pool deck, buffet or a nightclub can be noisy. Look at the deck plan and pick a cabin with cabins above and below it.
</Callout>

## Watch for these labels

- **Obstructed view** — lifeboats or structure block part of the window or balcony.
- **Guarantee cabin** — the line picks your cabin. Cheaper, and sometimes you get upgraded, but you give up control.
- **Connecting cabin** — great for families, but you may hear your neighbors through the door.

## Our quick picks

- **Budget couple:** inside cabin, midship.
- **First cruise, warm weather:** balcony, midship, mid-deck.
- **Family of four:** two connecting cabins, or a family-designed cabin if the ship has them.
- **Prone to seasickness:** low deck, midship, any cabin type.

<Verdict>
Pick midship on a mid-level deck, avoid cabins under busy public spaces, and only pay for a balcony if you'll actually sit on it.
</Verdict>
