Norwegian Cruise Line advertises Free at Sea on almost every sailing. It's a genuinely useful promotion, but "free" is doing a lot of work in that name.

## What Free at Sea includes

Free at Sea lets you pick from a set of perks when you book. Depending on the sailing and your cabin, these usually include:

- A beverage package
- Specialty dining meals
- Shore excursion credit
- Wi-Fi
- Discounted fares for the third and fourth guest in a cabin

**More at Sea** is NCL's bigger bundle, with more generous versions of those perks.

## What it costs

Two things to watch:

1. **Service charges on the perks.** You pay gratuities on the value of the beverage package and on dining. On a week-long cruise for two, that's real money, and it's charged even though the perk is "free."
2. **The fare itself.** Some fares are higher with perks than a fare without them. NCL often shows a cheaper "no perks" option, so always compare.

<Callout type="money" title="Do the math">
Add up what you'd actually spend on drinks, specialty dining and Wi-Fi without the perks. If that number is lower than the extra fare plus service charges, book without perks.
</Callout>

## Who wins with Free at Sea

- **Couples who drink every day** and want a couple of specialty dinners: almost always a good deal.
- **Non-drinkers:** pick the dining or excursion perks instead, and compare against the no-perks fare.
- **Families:** kids can't use the alcohol package, so focus on perks the whole cabin can use.

## Good news for 2027

Great Stirrup Cay, NCL's private island, is getting a major upgrade, and NCL has confirmed that Free at Sea and More at Sea drink packages will be honored on the island for 2027 sailings. [Here's what's changing there](/articles/great-stirrup-cay-2026).

<Verdict>
Free at Sea is great value for drinkers and foodies, and less so for everyone else. Always compare against the no-perks fare and budget for the service charges.
</Verdict>
