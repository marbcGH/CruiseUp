There's no single cheapest day to book a cruise, but there are patterns. Here's how to play them.

## Book early for choice

For popular ships, holidays and specific cabins (suites, family cabins, connecting rooms), booking 9–18 months out gives you the best choice. Prices on new ships often rise as they fill.

## Wave season

Many lines run their biggest promotions between January and March, often called "wave season." Expect perks such as onboard credit, reduced deposits or included extras rather than huge fare cuts.

## Last-minute deals

Within 60–90 days of sailing, after final payment deadlines, lines sometimes drop prices on unsold cabins. This works best if:

- You live near a homeport and don't need flights
- You're flexible about cabin type
- You're not traveling during school holidays

<Callout type="money" title="Keep watching after you book">
If the fare drops before your final payment date, ask your travel agent or the cruise line about repricing. Some fares are eligible for a lower price or onboard credit; non-refundable fares usually aren't.
</Callout>

## Our approach

1. **Book a refundable fare early** on the ship and dates you want.
2. **Set a price alert** or check weekly.
3. **Reprice** if it drops before final payment.
4. **Recheck add-ons too.** Drink packages and excursions go on sale often.

<Verdict>
Book early for the cabin you want, then keep watching. A refundable early booking you reprice later usually beats waiting for a last-minute deal.
</Verdict>
